Features

Everything Garage Buddy does, screen by screen.

Garage Buddy runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and in a web browser. Almost everything works with no account and no network. A few features use AI with an API key you supply yourself; those are marked AI key, and the app works fine without them.

Getting started

The first screen asks where your data should live:

  • On this device — no account, no sign-up, works offline. Included in your normal iPhone backup.
  • Google Drive — sign in with Google and the app keeps your records in a spreadsheet in your own Drive, which also lets your iPhone, iPad and Mac share one garage.

You can move between the two later; see Storage.

Then add your cars. Only a name is required, but each car can hold:

  • Year, make, model, colour, licence plate and VIN
  • Starting mileage — the odometer reading when you got the car. This matters for used cars, since miles are counted from here.
  • Date acquired and purchase price
  • A reference photo of the dashboard or interior, which helps AI scans work out which car a photo belongs to
  • Your own estimate of what it's worth (see Garage value)

Logging an entry

Every entry records a car, a date and an odometer reading, plus a category:

  • Odometer Check — just the mileage
  • Gas — with cost and gallons (or litres)
  • Charging — with cost and kWh
  • Maintenance, Repair, Insurance, Registration and Other — with cost

Any entry can also carry notes and attachments: a photo, a PDF, or a multi-page document scan saved as one PDF.

Checks as you type

  • The last reading for the car is shown under the odometer field. Enter a lower number and the app asks you to double-check it.
  • For gas and charging entries there is a “Missed a fill-up before this” switch. Turn it on when fuel went in that you didn't log — someone else filled up, or you lost the receipt — and that gap is left out of the economy figures instead of producing a wildly wrong MPG.

Getting to the entry screen quickly

  • The floating + button on the Dashboard and Garage
  • Siri, Spotlight and the Action Button — see Widgets, Siri and shortcuts
  • Home Screen quick actions: press and hold the app icon for Scan Odometer or Log Entry
  • The Scan Odometer widget

Retired cars drop out of the car picker so the list stays short.

Photo scanning

Odometer and gas pump

On the entry screen, tap Scan Odometer Photo (or Scan Gas Pump on a gas entry) and take or choose a photo. The number is filled in for you to confirm before saving.

  • On-device (the default) uses Apple's built-in text recognition. It's free, works with no signal, and the photo never leaves your phone. It also picks the car whose last reading is closest to, and just below, the number it read — a scan of 52,550 goes to the car last seen at 52,500, not the one at 10,000.
  • AI AI key handles harder photos: glare, odd angles, and the seven-segment digital displays that trip up ordinary text recognition. It identifies the car from your reference photos, the dashboard style and whether the mileage is plausible. The on-device reader runs alongside as a cross-check and flags any disagreement.

Receipts

Scan a receipt with the multi-page document scanner, or attach a photo or PDF you already have. AI key With AI set up, the app reads it and fills in the total, the date and the category, and writes a short summary into the notes. A PDF receipt is read the same way as a photographed one.

The dashboard

The Dashboard is the first tab. At the top are fleet totals, including Spent (all time). Below them is a chart with four views — tap a tab or swipe left and right to switch:

  • Mileage — one bar per car, the miles driven in the selected range, so the longest bar is the car you use most. Tap a bar to see that car month by month.
  • Total — every car's miles added together, one bar per month.
  • Expenses — one bar per car, what it cost you in the range. Tap a bar for that car's spending by month.
  • Economy — one bar per car. Tap for that car's individual fill-ups.

The range buttons — 1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, All — change the window for every chart. After tapping into one car, All cars next to the chart title takes you back.

Months with no reading yet are drawn as faded bars, projected from recent use. When a car is left off a chart — nothing logged in the range, or too few fill-ups — the chart says how many are missing and why, so a missing bar is never a mystery.

Under the chart are upcoming Reminders, which you can mark done from there, and Recent Activity.

The garage and each car

The Garage tab lists your cars in two groups, Active and Retired. Each row shows the car's photo, current mileage and small badges:

  • A bell when a reminder is due soon, and a bell with a badge when one is overdue
  • A checklist icon with the number of open to-dos
  • A warning icon for something that looks wrong in the log: an odometer reading that went backwards, a drop in fuel economy, an unusually large cost, or no entry for a long time
  • The car's estimated value, if you use Garage value

Tap a car to open its page, which has these sections:

  • Photos — the car's album, with one picture chosen as the cover
  • Mileage and a miles-per-month chart
  • Fuel Economy or Efficiency for electric cars — see below
  • Details — year, make, model, plate, VIN and so on
  • Reminders and To-Do
  • Value, and Purchase & Sale with the profit or loss once it's sold
  • Costs — cost per mile, total spent, and a breakdown by category
  • Documents, including a separate Previous Owner group
  • History — every entry, filterable by category

Selling or retiring a car

Retire (Sold) Car asks for the sale price and date and shows the profit against what you paid. Leave the price blank if it wasn't sold. A retired car keeps its full history and still appears in charts and reports; it just leaves the quick-entry picker. Reactivate Car brings it back.

Fuel economy, gas and electric

  • Per fill-up economy. Each bar is one tank, measured from the previous fill-up. A line shows the moving average over the last five, so a trend stands out from one odd tank.
  • Electric cars log Charging entries with kWh. Their page shows miles per kWh per charge, with the same five-charge average.
  • One fleet chart for both. On the Dashboard, electric cars are converted to MPGe using the EPA's figure of 33.7 kWh per gallon, so a hybrid, a V8 and an EV sit on one scale.

A car needs at least two gas entries with gallons, or two charging entries with kWh, before it has an economy figure.

Reminders and to-dos

Reminders

  • By date — registration, insurance, inspection. You get a notification at 9am on the day it's due.
  • By mileage — oil changes, tyre rotations. Checked against the car's latest reading.
  • One-off or repeating — every N months, or every N miles.

A reminder is marked Soon within 30 days or 500 miles, and Overdue after that. Marking a repeating reminder done moves it to the next due date or mileage that is still ahead of you, so catching up on an overdue one doesn't leave it overdue again.

Suggested service intervals AI key

On a car's page, Suggest Service Intervals asks the AI for that car's maintenance schedule. You get a list to review: untick anything you don't want, and suggestions that match reminders you already have start unticked. Nothing is added until you tap Add.

To-dos

For things you noticed and don't want to forget — a squeak to look at, a bulb to replace. No due date and no notification, just a list per car to tick off. The open count shows on the car's row in the Garage.

Documents and photos

Car documents

Titles, registrations, insurance cards and anything else that belongs to the car rather than to one entry. Add one by scanning (multi-page), taking a photo, or choosing a photo or PDF. From a document's menu you can rename, share, open it in Drive, or delete it.

  • Automatic naming AI key — the app reads a new document and suggests a name for it.
  • Extract Details AI key — reads the VIN, licence plate, insurance policy and expiry date off the document and offers to update the car and create a renewal reminder. You see exactly what will change, including any value it would replace, before anything is applied.
  • Previous owner records. Mark a document as coming from before you owned the car — old service invoices, an inspection report, the bill of sale. It's kept under Previous Owner as the car's history, is never counted as your mileage, and is included in the vehicle-history packet.

Photo album

Each car has its own album. Add pictures from the camera, your library, or the clipboard, and choose which one is the cover shown in the Garage list and at the top of the car's page.

Undo

Deleting a document, report or album photo removes it straight away and shows an Undo button for 8 seconds. The file is only deleted once that time runs out.

Garage value AI key

Garage Buddy can estimate what each car is worth from its year, make, model, colour and mileage. Tap Estimate Values in the Garage for the whole fleet, or Estimate Value on one car.

  • The Garage shows an estimated total, how many cars it covers (“8 of 9 cars”), and the change since the last estimate.
  • Enter your own figure on any car and it replaces the estimate everywhere, including the total. Worth doing for modified, rare or project cars, where a generic estimate is least reliable.
  • These are estimates, not appraisals.

Without an AI key this section doesn't appear at all.

Reports

The Reports tab is where every report is made. There are four:

Fleet report

For this month or this year: the numbers, where it went (spending by category, with the number of entries behind each total), and how much each car was driven and cost. A category that was really one purchase — a set of tyres — is labelled as such rather than looking like ongoing servicing.

AI key With AI set up, it also writes a plain-English summary. Without one, Save the Numbers as a Report keeps the figures on their own.

Garage report

A multi-page PDF of the whole fleet: an overview page with fleet totals and the miles-per-month chart, then a section per car with its stats and full log.

Car report

One car's complete history as a PDF, with its charts, entries and a list of its documents — the one to hand a mechanic.

Vehicle-history packet

The one to hand a buyer. It combines an AI-written history of the car AI key, the charts that back it up, the service record, the full log, photos, and the scanned receipts behind the work.

Because it's meant to leave your hands, it deliberately leaves out:

  • What you paid for the car and what you sold it for
  • Title, registration and insurance documents, which carry policy numbers and home addresses
  • Receipts filed under Insurance or Registration, for the same reason

All of that is still in the app and in the ordinary car report.

Saved reports

Every report you generate is kept. It's filed to a Reports folder in your Google Drive (or on the device in on-device mode) and listed under Saved reports. Tap one to read it again; press and hold to share it, rename it, open it in Drive or delete it. Report names start with the date — YYYY-MM-DD — what it is — which car — so the folder sorts in order. Generating a report works offline and uploads when you're back online.

Storage, sync and offline use

On this device

Everything stays in the app's own storage and goes into your normal device backup. No account and no network are needed. It doesn't sync between devices; iCloud sync for this mode is not available yet.

Google Drive

  • Your records live in a spreadsheet called Garage Buddy with tabs for Cars, Transactions, Attachments, Reminders and Todos. You can open it and edit it by hand: columns are matched by their header, so reordering them or adding your own won't break anything.
  • Photos, documents and receipts go into a Garage Buddy folder with a subfolder per car, and a Photo Album inside each.
  • The app asks only for Google's drive.file permission, which lets it see the files it created and nothing else in your Drive.
  • While the app is open it re-reads your data every 30 seconds, so a Mac window left open picks up what you just logged on your phone. Pull down on any list to refresh straight away.

Offline

In Google Drive mode your data is cached, so the app opens instantly and you can read everything with no signal. Entries you log offline are queued and sent when you're back online; a badge shows how many changes are waiting. New attachments need a connection to upload. If Google access expires, queued changes are kept and the badge offers to reconnect.

Moving your data

In Settings → Data:

  • Move Data to Google Sheets uploads your cars, entries, photos and documents to your Drive. Nothing is deleted until the move has finished.
  • Move Data to This Device copies everything onto the device and signs out of Google. The spreadsheet and files stay in your Drive as a snapshot.

Setting up AI

AI is optional and off until you add a key. Garage Buddy runs no servers and has no AI account of its own: you use your own key, and pay your provider directly. Set it up in Settings → AI.

  • Providers: Anthropic (Claude), Google (Gemini), or any OpenAI-compatible service.
  • Models: a short list for each provider with a note on what each is good at and a rough cost per scan — typically a fraction of a cent to a few cents. Or enter any model ID yourself.
  • Keys are stored in the iOS Keychain and sent only to the provider they belong to. Verify Key checks one works before you rely on it.
  • Test Models runs the full odometer scan on one of your own photos. Test All tries every model from every provider you have a key for, plus on-device, and compares the reading, confidence and time taken. Nothing is added to your log, but each AI run is billed to that key.

What AI is used for: odometer, gas pump and receipt scans; naming documents and extracting details from them; service interval suggestions; value estimates; and the written parts of the fleet report and vehicle-history packet. The privacy policy lists exactly what each one sends.

Widgets, Siri and shortcuts

Home Screen widgets

  • Scan Odometer (small) — one tap to the camera
  • Upcoming Reminders (small or medium) — what's due next
  • Fleet Snapshot (small or medium) — active cars, miles and spend this month, and a mileage trend line

Siri, Spotlight and the Action Button

Two shortcuts are available without any setup:

  • Scan Odometer — “Scan odometer in Garage Buddy” or “Log mileage in Garage Buddy”
  • Log Entry — “Log an entry in Garage Buddy”

Both show up in the Shortcuts app and Spotlight, and either can be assigned to the Action Button.

Units

In Settings → Units:

  • Distance: miles or kilometres
  • Volume: gallons or litres
  • Economy: MPG, km/L, L/100 km or gal/100 mi

Units only change what's displayed. The spreadsheet always stores miles and gallons, so switching never alters your data. Currency follows your device's region, and dates are shown as YYYY-MM-DD.

iPad and Mac

The same app runs on iPad and on Apple Silicon Macs. On the Mac, chart text is sized up for a desktop window, and reports open a normal save dialog. Where there's no camera, choose or paste photos instead.

To see the same garage on several devices, use Google Drive mode and sign in with the same Google account on each one.

On the web

web.garage-buddy.app is Garage Buddy in a browser, for when you're at a computer without the app. It works with the same Google Drive spreadsheet as the app: log a fill-up in the browser and it shows on your phone, and the other way round. Sign in with the Google account you use in the app.

It needs Google Drive mode. On-device data stays on the device that holds it, so to use the web version, first move your data to Google in the app (Settings → Data).

What it does

Most of the app, as of version 1.9.1:

  • The garage, each car's page, and the full log — add, edit and delete entries, cars, reminders and to-dos
  • The Dashboard charts — Mileage, Total, Expenses and Economy, with the same 1M to All ranges
  • The “Missed a fill-up” switch, and retiring sold cars
  • Receipts, documents and photos, including previous-owner records, with Undo on delete
  • Units, and a 30-second refresh while the page is open
  • Offline use: your data is cached in the browser, and entries made with no connection are sent when you're back online. Uploading files needs a connection.
  • Reports: every report the app saved to Drive is listed and can be opened, renamed or deleted. The web version also makes the fleet report's numbers and a car report as printable pages — print, or save as PDF.
  • Garage value: the app's estimates and garage total are shown, and you can set your own value here.
  • Install it from your browser (“Add to Home Screen” or “Install app”) and it opens in its own window.

What stays in the app

  • Anything that uses AI. The web version never calls an AI provider, so your API key never goes into a browser. Value estimates, document reading, service interval suggestions, the written fleet summary and the vehicle-history packet are made in the app; whatever they save to Drive then shows up on the web.
  • Photo scanning. Odometers, pumps and receipts are typed on the web.
  • Reminder notifications. Reminders still show on the Dashboard and each car's page, marked due soon or overdue.
  • Widgets, Siri and the Action Button.

How it handles your data

Like the app, the web version has no server of its own. The page signs in with Google in your browser and reads and writes your spreadsheet directly. It asks for the same narrow drive.file permission. Google's access token is kept only in memory and never saved; the browser remembers your email address so signing in again is quicker, and caches your garage so it opens instantly.
