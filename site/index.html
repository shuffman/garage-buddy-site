Garage Buddy

Mileage, fuel, and running costs — for every car you own.

Photograph the odometer. Garage Buddy reads it.

An iPhone, iPad and Mac app for people whose garage holds more than one set of keys. Snap the odometer to log it, watch what each car actually costs you, and get a nudge before service is due.

What it does

  • Log it in seconds. Photograph the odometer or the gas pump and Garage Buddy reads the numbers. On-device recognition is free, private, and works with no signal. Or start from a widget, Siri, or the Action Button.
  • See the real cost. Rank your cars by miles driven or money spent over any range, see cost per mile and spend by category, and follow fuel economy tank by tank — gas and electric on one chart.
  • Stay ahead of service. Reminders by date or by mileage, one-off or repeating, with a nudge the morning they are due. A to-do list per car for everything else.
  • Keep the paperwork. Scan receipts, titles and insurance cards to PDF, keep a photo album per car, and file the records that came from a previous owner.
  • Reports you keep. A monthly or yearly fleet summary, a PDF of the whole garage or one car, and a vehicle-history packet to hand a buyer — each one saved so you can read it again.
  • iPhone, iPad, Mac and the web. With Google Drive, the same garage on every device, refreshed while you look at it — and in any browser at web.garage-buddy.app.

Optional extras use AI with your own API key: tougher photo scans, reading details off documents, suggested service intervals, and value estimates. See every feature.

Your data stays yours

Choose where everything lives when you first open the app:

  • On your iPhone — completely offline. No account, no sign-up, nothing to configure.
  • In your own Google Drive — an ordinary spreadsheet you can open and edit yourself, so your records are never locked inside an app.

You can switch between the two later and take everything with you.

There are no servers, no ads, no subscription, and no account. Nothing is collected about you, because there is nowhere for it to go. The privacy policy spells out exactly what the app touches.
